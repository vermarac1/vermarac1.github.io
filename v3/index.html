Shyam Spectra · NaaS and Enterprise Connectivity

From consumer broadband to enterprise B2B

The challenge

Spectra was transitioning from a consumer broadband business to an enterprise connectivity company. The buyers were different, the sales cycle was different and the proposition was different. The existing consumer brand equity did not automatically transfer.

Marketing had no enterprise positioning, no ICP, no demand engine and no shared qualification process with sales.

What I did

  • Rebuilt the enterprise positioning and narrative
  • Defined the ICP from conversion data rather than assumption
  • Trained the team on the technology before launching any campaign
  • Realigned agencies and vendors around the enterprise proposition
  • Built a multi-channel GTM motion across outbound, content, paid, PR and events
  • Rebuilt marketing-to-sales qualification and handover
  • Built HubSpot segmentation and nurture
  • Established a fortnightly operating cadence across stakeholders
45%Lead generation 20%Sales conversion 7,000+Leads managed 35%Brand visibility 40%Retention
The lesson

A repositioning is a sequencing problem before it is a creative problem.

The business had to keep selling while its identity changed underneath it. The work was not simply about changing the message. It was about changing the organisation's ability to understand, market and sell the new proposition.

AgreeYa Solutions · Enterprise IT and SaaS · US Market

Building marketing around a $250K demand-generation mandate

The challenge

AgreeYa had traditionally relied on events, relationships and offline activity. Two acquired products, QuickApps and BeatBlip, needed a repeatable digital demand engine.

There was no mature funnel infrastructure, no established digital acquisition model and no shared marketing and sales qualification framework.

The approach

I started with the revenue target and worked backwards. Revenue, then opportunities, then SQLs, then MQLs, then leads, then channels, then budget.

The $250K was therefore not a request to do marketing. It was a model showing what the investment needed to produce at every stage of the funnel.

What I built

  • ICPs and personas for both products
  • Positioning and messaging
  • Website conversion journeys
  • SEO and content
  • LinkedIn Ads and paid search
  • Email nurture
  • Content syndication
  • Partner webinars and conferences
  • Outbound
  • Marketing and sales qualification
  • Funnel reporting and attribution
$250KBudget owned ~$1MRevenue contribution 1,000+Qualified leads / quarter 50%Lead quality 30%Conversion
The lesson

The most important outcome was not any individual channel.

It was that marketing moved from being a support function to having a measurable role in pipeline creation. Once the funnel maths existed, the budget conversation stopped being a negotiation and became a model.

Microsoft Ignite · AgreeYa Solutions

When the buyer is already in the building

The challenge

AgreeYa had attended Microsoft Ignite before. We had never sponsored it. The decision was simple: spend significantly more for one week and make the event accountable for a measurable pipeline outcome.

The bet

If the audience concentration was high enough, the event could become a high-intent acquisition channel rather than a branding exercise.

What I did

  • Built the campaign across pre-event outreach, the event itself and post-event nurture
  • Used the Microsoft Partner Network to reach attendees before the event
  • Booked sales meetings before the show opened
  • Built lead capture, CRM tracking and post-event follow-up before the event started
700+Leads vs 300 forecast 40+Target-account meetings ~$57Cost per lead 25%Brand awareness
The lesson

An event is only powerful when the audience concentration makes the event itself part of the qualification.

BlueKaktus · Positioning and Category

Repositioning an ERP company as an AI supply chain platform

The challenge

BlueKaktus was positioned as an ERP company. I believed the business needed to compete as an AI-powered supply chain platform instead.

That meant redefining the category, positioning, messaging, ICP, buyer personas, value proposition, competitive posture, commercial story and website architecture. The positioning had to address both sides of the market: brands, buying houses and retailers on one side, manufacturers and factories on the other.

What I did

  • Built the positioning strategy
  • Defined the messaging architecture
  • Built the ICP and personas for both sides of the market
  • Developed the commercial value story
  • Wrote the website content
  • Directed the agency
  • Led stakeholder alignment across founders, product and sales
  • Delivered against a fixed CEO-level deadline
The lesson

A senior marketing leader should be able to say more than "I increased leads." They should be able to say "I decided what the company should be saying to the market."

The new positioning and website went live in time for the CEO's Netherlands meeting. This is a judgement story rather than a percentage story, and judgement is the part that does not transfer from a playbook.

NayaOne · FinTech SaaS

Refilling a dry enterprise funnel with £3,000

The problem

The funnel had gone quiet. The obvious answer would have been to spend more. I did not think that was the problem.

The target market was small, high-value and risk-sensitive. I diagnosed it as an access and credibility problem rather than a budget problem.

The recommendation

I recommended content syndication as the channel, and going direct to TechTarget rather than routing the spend through an agency. That decision is what made the economics work. It removed the agency margin, let me negotiate the terms myself, and meant the deal could be structured around demonstrated intent instead of impressions.

What I did

  • Recommended content syndication over more paid spend, after diagnosing the constraint as access rather than budget
  • Went direct to TechTarget instead of through an agency, and ran the negotiation myself
  • Proposed the £3,000 programme to the CEO and got it signed off
  • Researched and wrote the white paper myself rather than briefing it out
  • Structured the commercial terms around demonstrated intent, not impressions
  • Routed the returned names straight to inside sales with the context attached
£3,000Total spend 1Asset, one partner, no agency 50Named intent-qualified prospects 2 monthsTo funnel movement
The lesson

In a small enterprise market you do not need more reach. You need better access to the people already signalling intent.

The value was not the budget. It was choosing the right channel, knowing which partner actually held the intent data, and going to them directly so the whole £3,000 bought named buyers rather than impressions and margin.

Point of view

How I think about marketing.

Six positions I arrived at by getting some of them wrong first.

01

Start with the business problem.

I do not start with channels. I start with the commercial objective, the buyer, the constraint and the number the business needs to move.

02

Position before promotion.

If the market does not understand the proposition, more media usually creates more noise rather than more demand.

03

Build the funnel as a system.

Acquisition, qualification, nurture, conversion and handover are connected. Optimising one while ignoring the rest rarely fixes the business.

04

Marketing and sales should speak the same language.

ICP, MQL, SQL, opportunity and revenue cannot mean different things to different teams, or the funnel maths stops being real.

05

Data should improve judgement, not replace it.

Analytics tells me where the system is breaking. Experience and judgement tell me what to investigate next.

06

AI should increase leverage.

I use it for research, audience intelligence, content velocity and campaign optimisation, while strategy and judgement stay human-led.

Artificial intelligence

AI is becoming part of the marketing operating system.

I see AI less as another marketing channel and more as a leverage layer across the function. It changes how much high-quality thinking a team can apply, not what good marketing is.

Audience intelligence

Research, segmentation, ICP refinement and account prioritisation.

Content systems

Research, ideation, content development, repurposing and velocity.

Campaign optimisation

Faster analysis, experimentation, targeting and performance insight.

Marketing operations

Automation, workflows, reporting and repetitive execution at scale.

Strategic thinking

Synthesising signals, spotting patterns and pressure-testing decisions.

The objective is not to replace marketing judgement. It is to increase the amount of high-quality judgement a marketing team can apply.
